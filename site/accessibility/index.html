Accessibility in Brickson

Last updated October 23, 2026

The short version: Brickson is made only of Apple's standard buttons, which Apple's accessibility features are built to use, so it needs no eye, head or switch tracking of its own. It is still in development and not yet on the App Store. This page lists what is in place today and how each part has been checked. Nothing has been checked on a real iPhone or iPad yet. Every check below is an automated test on Apple's iPhone and iPad simulators, or a reading of the code. A simulator cannot show how Eye Tracking, Switch Control, Dwell Control or sound behave in daily use.

How Brickson is built

  • Standard buttons only. Every control is one of Apple's standard buttons, with a name. Apple builds VoiceOver, Voice Control, Switch Control, Full Keyboard Access, AssistiveTouch, Head Tracking and Eye Tracking to reach buttons like these. Brickson has no eye, head or switch tracking of its own.
  • One choice speaks. A phrase speaks the first time you choose it. There is no confirm step.
  • Nothing moves on its own. Buttons are never reordered by how often or how recently you use them.
  • The screen stays on. The screen never locks while Brickson is open, because many people who use it cannot unlock their device.

How it's checked: Apple's automated accessibility audit runs on Home, and on Settings' first screen, Family Setup and the privacy policy, in light and dark, and on the emergency phrases, on Home while Get Attention sounds, and on Voice Settings, in light. Settings' other screens are not audited yet. A UI test taps a phrase once and checks that the tap starts it speaking; no test can hear the sound. A unit test checks the rule that keeps the screen on.

Ways to control Brickson

Touch

Yes, No, Emergency, Get Attention, Again and Settings are each at least 44 by 44 points, the size Apple's design guidelines ask for on a button. They stay inside the window, in the same place, as Home opens, while the emergency phrases show and after a phrase is spoken, at the default and the largest text sizes. While Get Attention sounds, Stop is at least 44 by 44 points and inside the window too, and no other button on Home moves as Stop appears and goes.

How it's checked: UI tests on iPhone and iPad simulators.

VoiceOver

Every control on Home has a name that VoiceOver reads. If a phrase is cut off, Get Attention cannot start or stops by itself, or the volume is very low, VoiceOver announces it without moving its place on the screen.

How it's checked: Apple's accessibility audit finds a name for every control. Unit tests check which warning is announced and its words; that VoiceOver's place does not move is from a reading of the code. A full run with VoiceOver on a real iPhone and iPad has not been done yet.

Voice Control

Each control on Home answers to the words it shows, so you can say "Tap Yes". Again also answers to "Repeat", Get Attention to "Help", and Stop to "Stop Alert". Settings answers to "Settings" even where it has room only for its gear. With the starter phrases, no two controls on Home share a name, even while Stop shows, so saying a control's name never matches two buttons.

How it's checked: unit tests of every name on Home. Voice Control has not been tried on a real device yet.

Switch Control and Full Keyboard Access

Brickson gives Switch Control, Full Keyboard Access and VoiceOver a fixed order. On Home, Yes, No, Emergency and Get Attention come first, then Again, then the message, then Stop while it shows, then the categories and Settings, then the phrases. Each of these areas is grouped, and the order is the same whatever is showing.

How it's checked: UI tests read the order Home gives these features, in every category and while Get Attention sounds, at the default and the largest text sizes. Whether VoiceOver, Switch Control and Full Keyboard Access follow it on a real device, and whether Switch Control scans each area as a group, has not been checked yet.

AssistiveTouch with Dwell Control, Head Tracking and Eye Tracking

These move a pointer, and with Dwell Control on, holding the pointer still taps. Brickson's controls are standard buttons for the pointer to rest on, and Brickson adds no tracking of its own. If a resting pointer, a switch or a second tap chooses the phrase that is still being spoken, Brickson lets it finish instead of starting it again, so the phrase does not stutter. Choosing Get Attention again while its tone plays leaves the tone playing. Stop is never the control nearest Get Attention. When Stop is chosen and goes, the control nearest the middle of where it was is a category, which only changes the phrases shown. The top part of that place is nearer Again (see Known limits).

How it's checked: unit tests of the repeat rule and of choosing Get Attention again. Unit tests also find the control nearest Get Attention, and nearest the middle of where Stop was, at every window width, with Bold Text on and off. UI tests do the same on iPhone and iPad simulators. A simulator cannot show dwell, head or eye pointing, so these wait for a real device.

Seeing and reading

Larger Text and Bold Text

Home's text follows the text size you set, up to the largest accessibility sizes. Yes, No, Emergency, Get Attention, Again, Stop, the categories and Settings make room for Bold Text; the phrases have not yet been measured with it. With the starter phrases and Bold Text off, no label on Home breaks a word in two: a label drops its icon first, then shrinks only as much as it must, and never gets smaller than it is at the default text size. Where Settings has little room, it shows only its gear.

How it's checked: UI tests at the default and the largest text sizes check that Home's controls stay in reach and in place, and at the largest size read the Yes, No, Emergency, Get Attention and Again labels back from screenshots to check that each word is drawn whole. Unit tests measure every starter label in its narrowest space, and the buttons named above with Bold Text on and off.

Contrast, Dark Mode and Increase Contrast

Home's colours are measured, never judged by eye: text at least 4.5 to 1 against what is behind it, and the edges of buttons at least 3 to 1, in light and dark, with Increase Contrast on and off.

How it's checked: unit tests measure every pair of colours. Apple's accessibility audit, which includes a contrast check, also runs on the screens listed under How Brickson is built.

Show Borders

With Show Borders on, every control on Home draws a border inside its edge. Pressing a control makes the border thicker, and the chosen category, and Emergency while its phrases show, keep an inner ring, so pressed, chosen and neither still look different.

How it's checked: unit tests of each border and ring, and of its contrast against every button colour. Not yet seen on a real device.

Not by colour alone

The chosen category, and Emergency while its phrases show, keep an inner ring, and a check mark where there is room, as well as their colour. VoiceOver says they are selected.

How it's checked: UI tests check that each category and Emergency is reported as selected once chosen, and unit tests check the ring. The check mark is from a reading of the code.

Reduce Motion

Home adds no animation of its own.

How it's checked: a reading of the code.

Per-App Settings

Brickson takes its text size, contrast and motion from iOS rather than from switches of its own, so settings you give Brickson alone, in Settings > Accessibility > Per-App Settings, are the ones it uses.

How it's checked: a reading of the code. Not yet tried on a real device.

Speech

Personal Voice

If you have made a Personal Voice and allow Brickson to use it, Brickson speaks your phrases in your own voice, unless another voice is chosen in Brickson's Voice Settings. iOS speaks the words; Brickson never receives the voice itself.

How it's checked: unit tests of how the voice is chosen. Nobody has heard Brickson speak on a real device yet.

When speech is cut off

If a phrase is cut off, for example by a phone call, Siri or unplugged headphones, Brickson shows "Stopped" on screen, and Again says the phrase again. A line on screen also says when the volume is very low. These words sit in space kept for them, so nothing else on the screen moves.

How it's checked: unit tests of when "Stopped" and the volume line show, and of Again. The kept space is from a reading of the code. Calls and headphones need a real device, so this has not been seen in use yet.

Get Attention

Get Attention and Stop

Get Attention is on Home in every state, directly under Emergency. Choosing it once starts a repeating tone to call someone into the room. Choosing it again while the tone plays leaves it playing. While the tone plays, Stop appears in a place of its own at the top left of the categories, away from Get Attention, and Stop ends the tone. No other button moves when Stop appears or goes. The line under the message says "Sounding", and VoiceOver reads it as "Get Attention is sounding." A warning, such as very low volume, takes that line first. If a phrase is chosen while the tone plays, the tone gets quieter until the phrase ends. Nothing flashes while the tone plays. Get Attention and Stop each have a colour of their own, and their words and icons say what each does.

How it's checked: UI tests on iPhone and iPad simulators, with a silent stand-in for the tone, choose Get Attention twice and then Stop. They check that Stop appears and goes, that the line under the message says the tone is sounding, that no other button moves, and that Apple's accessibility audit passes while the tone plays. Unit tests check where Get Attention and Stop sit at every window width, which warning comes first on the line, and that the tone gets quieter under a phrase, and they measure both colours. That nothing flashes is from a reading of the code. Nobody has heard the tone on a real device yet. So it has not been checked how loud it is, or whether it can be heard from another room, with the silent switch on, or with the device locked. "Sounding" means Brickson is playing the tone, not that anyone has heard it.

When Get Attention fails

If the tone cannot start, the line under the message says "Alert failed". If it stops by itself, for example because of a phone call, Siri or unplugged headphones, Stop goes and the line says "Alert stopped". Brickson never starts the tone again by itself, and VoiceOver announces each failure. Choosing Get Attention again tries again. These words sit in the space kept under the message, so nothing else on the screen moves.

How it's checked: unit tests of when "Alert failed" and "Alert stopped" show, of what VoiceOver is told, of trying again, and that the tone never restarts by itself. That Stop goes, and the kept space, are from a reading of the code. Calls, Siri and headphones need a real device, so this has not been seen in use yet.

Known limits today

  • Nothing has been checked on a real iPhone or iPad. Nobody has yet used Brickson with Eye Tracking, Switch Control, Dwell Control or Voice Control, heard it speak, or heard the Get Attention tone.
  • Home's sizes and spacing are the same for every way of controlling it. They have not yet been measured for pointing with the eyes or head, or for switches.
  • The top part of the place where Stop appears is nearer Again than any other button, and Again says the last phrase again. With Dwell Control, a pointer resting there while Stop is not showing could repeat the last phrase. A unit test finds this in Home's layout; how Dwell Control picks the nearest button has not been checked on a device.
  • On an iPhone at the larger accessibility text sizes, and at any text size with Display Zoom set to Larger Text, the phrases can need scrolling. They also need it from the default text size up on an iPhone SE, and on an 11-inch iPad or an iPad mini in landscape. Those three are worked out from measurements of the layout and have not been seen running yet.
  • In Dark Mode, when an iPad shows Brickson beside another app, the system can lighten the background, and the edges of the coloured buttons other than Get Attention and Stop may then measure a little under 3 to 1 (about 2.8 to 3.0, against a commonly reported background colour; the tests do not measure it yet). Against that colour, Get Attention's edge comes to about 3.4 to 1 and Stop's to about 3.1 to 1. With Show Borders on, each button's border keeps its edge clear.
  • Get Attention and Stop are on Home only, not in Settings. While Settings is open, choose Back to Talking to reach them. If the tone fails or stops while Settings is open, the line at the foot of Settings says "No alert — Back".
  • While "Alert failed" or "Alert stopped" shows, another warning, such as "Stopped" for a phrase cut off or very low volume, does not show on that line until Get Attention plays again. VoiceOver still announces each one.
  • At the largest text sizes, the ring around the chosen category can touch the first or last letter of its name. The test that reads labels back from screenshots does not check the categories yet.
  • Brickson is in English only.
